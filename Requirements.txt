Gap 1 — Lack of Association in Scene Understanding (Spatial + Temporal + Semantic)

Current computer vision systems handle spatial, temporal, and semantic information in isolation. A model might be excellent at detecting where an object is (spatial), but it cannot simultaneously reason about when that object appeared relative to another (temporal) or what role it plays in the overall activity (semantic). Human perception is inherently associative — we do not see "a knife" and "a hand" separately; we instantly understand "someone is cutting." No existing general framework fuses all three cues in a unified representation. Your related work table confirms this: the 2025 Video Refer Suite handles spatial-temporal but is data-heavy; the 2022 semantic segmentation work handles semantic only; GCN (2019) handles spatial only. None fuses all three simultaneously for general activity understanding.

Gap 2 — Insufficient Temporal Interaction Modeling

Traditional models like ResNets and standard ViTs operate on individual frames or fixed clips. They cannot capture how relationships between objects change over time — e.g., a bowl being empty, then being filled, then being used. The Temporal Relation Networks (TRN, 2018) in your related work acknowledged this but remained permutation-invariant, meaning they cannot distinguish strict event order when visual content looks similar. This is a well-documented shortcoming that directly limits activity recognition accuracy in real-world, long-horizon scenarios. Without temporal interaction modeling, you cannot distinguish "person picked up knife then cut vegetables" from "person held knife while vegetables were already cut" — which are semantically very different actions.

Gap 3 — Lack of a Universal/Generalisable Framework

Most state-of-the-art systems are engineered for a narrow domain. Your related work table directly illustrates this: the 2024 Embodied Spatial Reasoning benchmark is robotics-focused; the 2022 semantic segmentation work targets autonomous driving; the 2025 Video Refer Suite depends on a proprietary 700K-sample dataset. This domain specificity creates a fragmentation problem — the industry must rebuild scene understanding pipelines from scratch for every new application. The lack of a domain-agnostic framework imposes significant engineering cost and prevents transfer learning across domains like surveillance, healthcare robotics, and smart city infrastructure. Your thesis directly addresses this by proposing a generic framework with a modular architecture designed for extensibility.

Gap 4 — Fragmented Modeling of Objects and Activities

Object detection and activity recognition have historically been treated as two separate research tracks. Object detectors (YOLO, ResNet, ViT) output bounding boxes and class labels; activity recognizers take those labels as post-processed input. This pipeline introduces an information bottleneck — rich visual features computed during detection (texture, relative positions, motion blur) are lost before activity recognition begins. Your framework addresses this by fusing detection and activity recognition within a shared representational space, enabling the activity recognition module to draw on raw feature maps rather than only final detection outputs.
